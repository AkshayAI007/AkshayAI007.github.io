THE PROBLEM

Risk hides in noisy, imbalanced data.

Cardiovascular disease is one of the leading causes of death worldwide, yet the clinical signals that predict it — blood pressure, cholesterol, smoking history, BMI, age — are noisy, correlated, and skewed: the vast majority of patients in any dataset never go on to develop disease. A model optimised for raw accuracy will happily predict "healthy" for everyone and still score well, while missing the patients who actually need attention.

THE APPROACH

Engineer for recall, not vanity metrics.

Working from the Framingham Heart Study dataset, the pipeline runs structured EDA and feature engineering (pulse pressure, BMI banding, age interactions), corrects the class imbalance with SMOTE so the minority "at-risk" class isn't drowned out, and benchmarks several classifiers before settling on a tuned XGBoost model — selected specifically for the precision/recall trade-off that matters in a screening context.

THE USP

The model is tuned for the cost of a missed diagnosis, not for a leaderboard score — trading a small amount of precision for a recall of 0.85, so fewer at-risk patients slip through.
