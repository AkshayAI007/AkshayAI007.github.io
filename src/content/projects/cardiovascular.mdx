<Column kicker="THE PROBLEM" title="Risk hides in noisy, imbalanced data.">
Cardiovascular disease is one of the leading causes of death worldwide, yet the clinical signals that predict it — blood pressure, cholesterol, smoking history, BMI, age — are noisy, correlated, and skewed: the vast majority of patients in any dataset never go on to develop disease. A model optimised for raw accuracy will happily predict "healthy" for everyone and still score well, while missing the patients who actually need attention.
</Column>
<Column kicker="THE APPROACH" title="Build the pipeline so the score can be trusted.">
A stratified 60/20/20 split keeps the test set untouched until the end. Feature engineering adds pulse pressure and drops collinear inputs; SMOTE oversamples the training fold only. Nine classifiers are tuned with GridSearchCV on AUC, the winner is Platt-calibrated so a “30% risk” means roughly 30%, and the decision threshold is tuned on validation by F2 — recall weighted twice over precision, with a precision floor.
</Column>
